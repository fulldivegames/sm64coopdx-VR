SM64 CO-OP DX VR - CURRENT LOCAL QUEST PLAYER GUIDE
================================================

WHAT YOU NEED

- A Meta Quest 3 with Developer Mode enabled
- Your own legally obtained, unmodified Super Mario 64 US ROM
- A PC or Android phone for installing the APK

Nintendo ROMs and game assets are not included in this download.
The ROM and extracted Nintendo assets are never included in the APK.


This guide describes the current local build, not a new published release.
For current physical gestures, see PHYSICAL-SWIMMING.txt and PHYSICAL-JUMPING.txt.
Propeller Mushroom: 60 seconds. Jump again in the air for a burst; hold crouch
for a fast drill, release for slow glide. See PROPELLER-MUSHROOM.txt or the
in-game Special Moves tutorial. Ordinary twirling has no crouch drill.
The first 0.9.2 launch enables physical jumping, swimming, punching, grabbing,
climbing, swing release and ground/air motion dives once. Crouching and bindings
are unchanged. Disable any unwanted actions in settings; later launches retain
your changes. Other settings remain preserved.

FIRST-TIME SETUP

1. Install the APK with SideQuest or ADB.
2. Launch SM64 Co-Op DX VR Standalone from Unknown Sources.
3. Use the system file picker to select your legally obtained US .z64 ROM.
4. Wait for validation and asset preparation to finish.
5. Open Camera Settings if you want to adjust first-person height or comfort.

The game remembers the validated ROM in its user-data folder.


CAMERA MODES

- Third Person Mode keeps the character visible. It works with the original
  camera and SM64 Co-Op DX's free camera. Camera distance is adjustable.
- First Person Mode is the default. The headset controls the view and Mario's
  forward direction. The camera stick provides smooth horizontal turning.
- First-person movement supports normal forward steering up to 90 degrees,
  full-speed direction-locked movement around the complete stick circle, a
  steerable reverse jog, and a short forward-to-back side-flip window. Mario's
  body stays locked to the selected HMD/controller direction while horizontal
  travel steers smoothly at Mario's native turning rate and landing momentum
  remains independent.
  The forward prime lasts 12 gameplay simulation ticks and the backpedal
  side-flip grace lasts 10 gameplay simulation ticks. These are Mario's
  30 Hz updates, not headset render frames, so Quest refresh rate does not
  shorten either window.
- Button swimming, Wing Cap flight, and shell riding retain their normal VR
  steering. Optional Physical Swimming uses strokes with headset aiming and
  an overhead upward-swim gesture. Normal action buttons remain available.
- Tunnels, conversations, doors, and other scripted camera events continue
  running without pulling the first-person headset view away from the player.
- The character's head and normal arms are hidden in first person. Tracked,
  player-colored gloves remain visible. Torso and legs can be toggled.


CANNONS

1. Enter a cannon normally.
2. When aiming begins, the virtual view aligns horizontally with the cannon's
   forward firing angle.
3. Move your head to aim and press Jump (Right Primary by default) to fire.

Head movement remains free, but the shot stays inside the cannon's original
firing cone. Looking outside that cone gradually darkens the view. A rotating
Mario camera-arrow points back toward the nearest valid firing direction.


DEFAULT VR CONTROLS

Move                    Left stick
Smooth turn / camera    Right stick
Jump                    Right Primary (usually A)
Attack / interact       Right Secondary (usually B)
Crouch                  Left Trigger
Other actions           See Controller Bindings for current mappings
Pause                   Left stick click
Close fist / grab       Hold that hand's Grip
Right Trigger punch     Disabled by default; optional in Controller Bindings

Open Settings > VR > Controller Bindings to remap the sticks and buttons.
A connected gamepad can remain active alongside the VR controllers.


PHYSICAL ACTIONS

PUNCH
Hold a grip to close that glove, then make a deliberate punch. The tracked
fist is the attack point. Mario's body does not have to face the target.

GRAB AND THROW
Close either grip while the glove overlaps something the game allows Mario
to pick up, such as a Bob-omb, Mips, or a baby penguin. Keep holding to carry
it at your hand. Release gently to drop it, or release during a faster hand
movement to throw it.

DIVE
Punch forward with both hands at nearly the same time. Air dives require a
20 cm stroke at 1.65 m/s; ground dives use 13 cm at 1.20 m/s. Headset yaw
sets forward, not its pitch. Jump gestures take priority, with no added settle timer.
In the air this performs a
dive. While running fast enough, it performs a ground running dive. The two
types can be enabled separately in Motion Control Settings.

CROUCH AND GROUND POUND
With Physical Crouching / Ground Pounds enabled, lower your headset by about
10% of your calibrated standing height by default. Physical Crouch Depth,
right below the checkbox in Immersion > Movement & Body, ranges from 10 to
50%; lower values require less crouching. Mario stays crouched until you
stand back up. Crouching while airborne triggers a ground pound.

BOWSER
Reach either glove to Bowser's tail and hold that grip. Move the held hand in
a turning arc around your body to build spin speed, then release the grip to
throw. The camera stick can also help turn. Spin acceleration and maximum
speed are adjustable.

HOOT AND MOVING ACTORS
Grip a supported interaction point to attach to it. Release the grip to let
go. Physical interactions use the game's normal interaction and networking
paths where possible.

PHYSICAL CLIMBING
Close a grip while the matching glove touches a native pole, tree, or
hangable surface. Pull or move your hands to climb. For a ledge, physically
lift or move your headset over the top of the ledge, then let go of the grip
to climb up onto it. The optional cheat permits deliberate fresh-grip wall
and ceiling contacts, but does not turn empty air or boxes into climbables.


VR SETTINGS

Camera Settings
  Camera mode, distance, per-character height, forward/back position,
  movement calibration, and field of view.

Controller Bindings
  Stick selection, press-to-bind button mapping, and optional
  Right Trigger punching.

Motion Control Settings
  Punching, grabbing, motion dives, punch sound, punch requirements,
  collider length, and Bowser spin tuning.

Model Settings
  Body Settings and Hand Settings. Body Settings includes torso/legs, the
  optional Feet Only view, crawling and mounted-action visibility, and body
  placement. Hand Settings contains glove size, rotation, and positions.

Performance
  Headset render scale. Lower it if a dense level cannot maintain a
  comfortable frame rate; menus remain at full resolution.

Cheats
  Flying Speed (%) scales VR Wing Cap flight from 100% to 300%. The multiplier
  is applied to the native flight velocity and does not accumulate each frame.
  Swimming Speed also ranges from 100% to 300%; Running Speed is capped at
  200%. Both remain at their native 100% defaults.

HUD Settings
  HUD opacity, corner spread, positioning and attachment options. Speedrunning
  has its own VR menu for the timer and split viewer.

Experimental
  Flat first person, True First Person, True Diving (Camera Effect), Arms
  Mode, and original Mario movement.

Immersion
  Crouch / Sand Camera, Face-Stuck Blackout, Cannon Aim Direction Cone,
  Head-Tracked 3D Sound, Side-Flip Camera Follow, 180 Degree Wall-Jump Camera
  Turn, Physical Crouching / Ground Pounds, and optional Camera on Body During
  Climb Up. The climb-up camera option is disabled by default.

Effects
  Optional Normal Maps are disabled by default. Enable Add Normal Maps to apply
  matching texture normals; strength and gloss are adjustable in this menu.
  Twirl Tornado Effect is enabled by default. During Shy Guy, Spindrift, and
  Tweester twirls, a small collisionless rotating white tornado remains at the
  character's feet for the full action. It is 25% transparent and scales to
  roughly 75% of the selected character.

True First Person follows animated body movement and might cause sickness.
True Diving follows the animated dive position only during dives and may also
be uncomfortable. Both options are disabled by default.
Every submenu has a Set to Defaults button.


CAP GESTURE

During a successful star exit, bring either tracked glove to the headset or
just above it and hold that hand's Grip + Trigger. Either hand can pull off the
cap and hold it for about one minute. Let go to drop the cap onto level
geometry; it rests briefly and fades. Death and Exit Course do not activate the successful-exit gesture.
With Grab Cap at Any Time enabled, the same Grip + Trigger gesture works
outside successful exits. A red X on the life icon marks a missing hat.


STARTUP SHADER CACHE

The game prepares common shaders when it starts. New shader definitions found
during play are stored in gfx_shader_cache.txt and prepared on later launches.
This is intended to reduce repeat stutters in levels and mods you have used.


KNOWN LIMITATIONS

- This build targets Quest 3 standalone and remains experimental.
- Online co-op, mods, and long play sessions need more standalone testing.
- True First Person, True Diving, and Arms Mode are experimental.
- The 3D star models do not currently appear on the Star Select screen in VR.
- Some original flat billboard objects can look unusual in stereo VR.


TROUBLESHOOTING

- If the app cannot find its files after reinstalling, select the ROM again.
- For diagnostics, connect the Quest with ADB and capture Android logcat.

- Include your headset, OpenXR runtime, GPU, reproduction steps, and relevant
  [VR] or [GFX] console lines when reporting a problem.

CURRENT LOCAL GESTURES AND SETTINGS

VR > Immersion > Movement & Body:
- Physical Swimming defaults on for new settings. Reach outward, then pull water back
  toward your upper torso. Normal pulls follow headset aim. Pull down from
  overhead to swim upward. Recovery produces no thrust. Either hand,
  alternating hands and both hands work. Normal button swimming remains.
- Physical Jumping defaults on for new settings. Hold Grip and punch within 15 degrees
  of straight up while grounded. Trigger is not needed. Either/both hands can
  jump; keep one raised to hold Jump, or lower/release to shorten it. Lower and
  punch again after landing for normal double/triple jumps.
  "Use Triggers Instead of Grips" switches only physical jumping (including
  wall kicks) to either trigger. Off by default; change your crouch binding
  before enabling it. Grabbing and punching still use grips.
  Hard physical climb-release swings get the full jump-off ascent; softer
  swings retain the shorter release. Simply letting go still drops normally.
- Physical Crouching / Ground Pounds is on by default. Physical Crouch Depth
  defaults to 10%, adjustable from 10 to 50. Lower values need less crouching.
  Head-tilt compensation reduces accidental crouching when looking down.
  The setting uses calibrated height; recenter while standing neutrally.
- Existing saved settings are retained. New defaults do not overwrite them.

Special power-up spawn weights range from 1 to 100, default 50. These are
relative weights, not literal percentages. Disable a power-up with its checkbox.
Big Hands lasts 30 seconds; its optional 60-second timer applies at pickup.
Big Hands has half the relative box-spawn weight of ordinary power-ups at
equal slider settings, even with its optional 60-second timer enabled.
Physical Jumping also wall-kicks; its 120 ms landing buffer helps jump chains.
Keep the triggering fist raised for a full-height wall kick; lower/release it
to shorten the kick. Landing clears the gesture hold.
Crossed Tree Billboards defaults on; Look Toward Spawned Star defaults off.
Normal maps remain optional and off by default.

Controller Bindings captures the next chosen input. Right-primary still
confirms menus even if Jump is remapped. Pause defaults to left stick click.
Mic starts dictation; press it again to finish, rather than cancel. Closing the
keyboard cancels. Quest uses Android dictation; PC uses its bundled speech files.

Speedrunning has its own VR menu for setup, names, split count, Confirm, HUD
position/size/color and progressive split reveal. See VR-SPEEDRUN.txt.
Special > Filters includes Cell Shaded and Super Mario Land. Character Select
uses a floating theater panel. See PHYSICAL-SWIMMING.txt and PHYSICAL-JUMPING.txt
for gesture details. These are local-build instructions, not a release notice.
Propeller Mushroom lasts 60 seconds: jump again in the air (button or physical
jump gesture) for one burst per airtime. Hold crouch for fast descent and a
3x-speed opaque tornado; release to glide. Ordinary twirling has no crouch drill.
Its helmet follows the head and is hidden in first person. Customize/export
the Propeller palette: Cap = helmet, Emblem = rotor. See PROPELLER-MUSHROOM.txt.
The Hammer Suit helmet and back shell follow Mario's animated body. A top stomp on a normally stompable
enemy relaunches Propeller Mario at full burst strength; side hits and Bullies do not.

POWER STAR
----------
Collect the half-size bouncing star with your body, headset or hand. It retains
its eyes and leaves golden sparkles. For 30 seconds, Mario shimmers with rainbow
colors, runs 1.5x as fast, jumps 1.5x higher, and attacks enemies on contact
without taking enemy-contact damage. One-hit enemies are defeated; Chain Chomps
explode. Falling out of the level and scripted deaths still apply.
The rainbow tint and dedicated Super Mario Bros. 3 Star Theme fade during the
last four seconds; abilities remain active until expiry. The Spawn Menu drops
it from the same high position as other power-ups. It bounces on floors (including
lava and quicksand) and on water approached from above, rather than sinking.

Special Moves offers Power Star 60-Second Timer. Set it before pickup; changing
it during a run does not change the active timer. Power Star has half the spawn
weight of ordinary powers at the same slider setting. It has its own enable
checkbox and weight slider, plus a Spawn Menu entry. Another power replaces it.
In Power-Up Spawn/Enable, Power-Ups, Specials and Gestures are labeled sections,
not separate menus. Big Hands and Power Star are adjacent under Specials; their
weights are half the ordinary rate. Rasengan / Rasen-Shuriken have no spawn weight.

MUSIC PRIORITY
--------------
Native Wing, Metal and Vanish caps retain their original cap themes. Sonic Shoes
and Power Star retain their dedicated songs with Alternate Power-Up Music on or
off. Alternate music affects only added powers without a dedicated track.
The Power-Up Music switch controls added power-up songs, not native cap music.
