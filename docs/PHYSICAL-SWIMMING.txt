Physical Swimming (PC VR and standalone Quest)

On by default for new settings. Toggle VR Settings > Immersion > Movement & Body > Physical Swimming.
The first 0.9.2 launch enables physical actions once (not crouching). You can
disable them again; subsequent launches preserve that choice.
Normal strokes propel you in the direction the headset is looking.
Reach outward to recover, then pull water backward/downward. Pulls remain
valid beside and behind your torso; a 4 cm recovery re-arms the next stroke.
Each productive pull adds thrust in that direction; looking down lets you dive.
Existing drift does not rotate when you look around. Recovery adds no thrust.
Either hand, alternating hands, and both hands use independent stroke state.
Existing button swimming remains available.

For upward swimming while looking forward, raise your hands overhead and pull
downward within 20 degrees of vertical. That power phase propels you upward.
Normal surface strokes follow headset direction; either hand can take a turn.
The shallow-water camera remains lowered by 30 game units, fading out at depth.
Normal water collisions, surface limits, currents and buoyancy still apply.

Strength is unchanged from the last test: 1.6575 times the initial implementation.
The Swimming Speed setting remains active. Holding objects, metal-cap movement,
physical climbing, cutscenes, menus and death do not generate strokes.
Recenter, tracking loss, level transitions and discontinuities reset the detector.

Implementation: two fixed-size per-hand states updated once per game tick.
Pose-to-pose controller travel uses a gameplay camera rotation, independently of
render-only glove transforms and scene traversal. No glove offsets affect thrust.
Instantaneous runtime velocity is not required, so slow pulls and strokes that
finish between game ticks still contribute. Head motion alone adds no travel.
There are no new scene traversals or collision queries. A productive pull needs
4 cm of travel; impulse per stroke and total drift speed remain capped.
Synthetic regression tests cover direction, recovery, curved pulls, repeated
strokes and invalid tracking. Real gesture consistency still needs headset testing.
